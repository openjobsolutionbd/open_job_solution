## বর্তমান তথ্য

In 2017, 'fake news' became Collins Dictionary's word of the year and it's remained in the headlines ever since. According to the Oxford Dictionary 'disinformation' means 'false information that is given deliberately'. Disinformation is false or misleading information that is intentionally created and spread to deceive people, influence opinions or cause harm; unlike mistakes or rumors shared unknowingly (misinformation). Deepfake belongs to the 'tools or methods of spreading disinformation' section. Deepfake is a specific AI-based technology used to create highly realistic but fake videos, images or audio.

### Origination of Disinformation & Deepfake

The term 'disinformation' originates from the Russian word 'dezinformatsiya' first widely used during the Soviet era. It referred to a state-controlled strategy of deliberately spreading false or misleading information to deceive foreign governments, manipulate public opinion and advance political objectives. The term 'deepfake' was first used in late 2017 on the online digital conduit Reddit by a user with the username 'deepfakes.' This person and others in the Reddit community began sharing AI-generated videos that used deep learning techniques to persuasively plausible swap the faces of celebrities into other videos (often explicit content), making highly realistic but fake media. The word deepfake itself combines 'deep' (from deep learning, a form of artificial intelligence) and 'fake' (because the media is deliberately falsified).

### Root Causes of Disinformation

Several factors contribute to the emergence of disinformation sprees:

- **Political Agendas** – Governments, parties and political groups often use disinformation to orchestrate coercive elections, policies or public perception.
- **Social and Cultural Divisions** – Pre-existing tensions, such as ethnic, religious or ideological divides, make societies more susceptible to the rapid spread of falsehoods.
- **Profit Motives** – Hyperbolically dramatized content, clickbait and fake news generate revenue from ads, web traffic and exponential transmissibility.
- **Psychological Biases** – Humans are drawn to information that evokes fear, anger or surprise, making false stories more likely to spread.
- **Crisis Situations** – Wars, pandemics, and natural disasters create information gaps, which are exploited by manipulators.
- **Low Media Cognitive Proficiency** – A lack of critical skills among users increases susceptibility to deceptive content.

### Instances of Disinformation & Deepfake

Real-world incidents clearly illustrate the risks posed by these technologies. During the COVID-19 pandemic, misleading videos circulated online falsely claiming that vaccines contained microchips or led to infertility. In election periods, deepfake clips have shown politicians supposedly making inflammatory remarks, raising concerns about interference in democratic processes. In conflict zones, manipulated war footage has been used as propaganda, exaggerating events or misrepresenting casualty figures to shape public perception. The entertainment world has also been targeted through celebrity hoaxes, where fake videos spread false news about deaths or scandals. Similarly, financial misinformation—such as fabricated reports about companies or markets—has triggered economic losses and panic. Together, these examples show how deepfakes and disinformation waves can influence cognitive judgments, distort perceptions of reality and create serious real-world consequences.

### Consequences of Disinformation

The impacts of disinformation sprees are severe:

- **Erosion of Trust** — Citizens lose confidence in media, governments and experts.
- **Societal Ideological Bifurcation** — Communities split along ideological or social lines.
- **Democratic Threats** — Elections and political processes are orchestrated coercively.
- **Public Health Risks** — False medical claims can lead to avertable through intervention illness or deaths.
- **Economic Damage** — Companies and markets can suffer due to rumors or fake statistics.
- **Psychological Impact** — Anxiety, confusion, and fear proliferate.
- **Incitement of Violence** — False information can trigger riots, protests or targeted attacks.

### Sharp Rise in AI-Driven Fake News

Researchers and analysts say that the use of AI-generated videos increase during elections and these videos can influence voters' opinions. As an election approaches, political campaigns, propaganda and communication through social media, Facebook, YouTube, etc. increase. Experts say that alongside, AI-generated fake videos, misleading information and manipulated content are also spread. In many cases, old footage is edited, voices are changed or fake scenes are added to make the videos look real. Because of advanced editing and AI tools, it is becoming difficult for ordinary viewers to distinguish real from fake. Researchers and analysts believe these AI-generated videos can directly influence voter behavior. Such videos are often created to create confusion, damage reputations or provoke emotional reactions among voters.

### How to spot a deepfake

Information and communication technology experts say that public awareness must be increased to combat this trend. Some top tips for spotting a deepfake by disinformation experts:

- **Features** – Take a closer look at the person's fingers and face in the video. Do their eyes look like they do in other pictures? Have they got the right number of fingers?
- **Fact-check** – When are people claiming that this picture or video was taken? Does the weather match up with the day or the location?
- **Movement** – Does the person look like they're moving like a cartoon, rather than a real person? Do they sound a bit like a robot rather than a person?
- Remember to question the things you see on the internet – particularly if you think they look a bit strange or unusual.

Disinformation has evolved from simple propaganda into a sophisticated digital threat, with deepfakes making false content appear convincingly real. As elections near, AI-generated videos and manipulated media can rapidly influence public opinion, distort political narratives, and weaken democratic trust. The effects go beyond politics, fueling social division, economic harm, and public confusion. Because deepfakes provide visual 'evidence,' they make deception more persuasive and harder to detect. Combating this challenge requires stronger media literacy, advanced detection tools, ethical AI use, platform accountability and critical public awareness. Protecting truth in the digital age is now a shared responsibility essential for democratic stability.
